# Current Speaker of Ghana’s Parliament — official-source research

**Research cut-off:** 2026-10-13  
**Access date for sources below:** 2026-10-13  
**Subject:** Rt. Hon. Alban Sumana Kingsford Bagbin (also rendered “Alban Kingsford Sumana Bagbin”, “Alban S.K. Bagbin”, “Alban Sumana Bagbin”, and, in one 2026 news item, “Bagbink”).  
**Scope note:** This is a traceable, partial investigation of publicly accessible sources, not a claim that every page or parliamentary record on the domain was crawled. The initial 32-source/19-event snapshot is preserved in `ghana_speaker_dataset.json`; `ghana_speaker_enrichment.json` records the additive research pass. The live database now contains 61 unique sources and 48 events.

## A. Executive summary

The official Parliament of Ghana Office of the Speaker page names **Rt. Hon. Alban Kingsford Sumana Bagbin** as Speaker from **7 January 2021 “to date.”** Parliament’s live site also labels him Speaker in the Ninth Parliament, and its news archive contains a ruling issued by him on 29 September 2026. He is therefore the current Speaker as of the research cut-off. The Office page describes the Speaker as the House’s presiding officer and Chairperson of the Parliamentary Service Board.

Parliament-hosted records support a career reaching back to the beginning of the Fourth Republic: a 2025 Parliament news item says Bagbin has been in the Chamber since 1993; the directly processed Electoral Commission 2004 results list his Nadowli West NDC candidacy with 11,296 votes (50.5%); and the indexed Parliament-hosted 2008 results report 12,997 votes (61.9%). A directly inspected Modern Ghana table reports a conflicting 12,902 (61.93%), so that total remains unresolved. In a 2025 Hansard item, the incoming Majority Leader attributed to Bagbin a previous path through Minority Leader, Majority Leader, an executive role, Deputy Speaker, and Speaker. The accessible sources do **not** supply a complete authoritative term-by-term biography, all election returns, or a primary record of his Speaker-election vote.

As Speaker, the records located document procedural decisions and institutional activity, including the 2024 Standing Orders orientation, a 2025 formal communication concerning the Appointments Committee, a 2025 speech in Rabat, a 2026 address on decentralisation, a 2026 Supreme Court anniversary lecture, and the September 2026 ruling that a proposed narcotics-seizure inquiry motion was inadmissible. The latter is a Speaker’s decision reported by Parliament; this report does not independently assess its merits.

## B. Identity and biographical/political profile

### Identity verified

The Parliament Office of the Speaker history lists **“Rt. Hon. Alban Kingsford Sumana Bagbin”** with tenure beginning 7 January 2021 and continuing “to date” [SRC-002]. Current parliamentary news uses the fuller form **“Rt. Hon. Alban Sumana Kingsford Bagbin”** [SRC-017]. The official repository uses **“Bagbin, Alban Sumana Kingsford”** as author [SRC-016]. These are name-order variants, not separate people. A typo “Bagbink” appears in the September 2026 report [SRC-017]; the same item repeatedly uses Bagbin, so it is treated as a typo.

### Constituency and party

The downloaded Electoral Commission 2004 results directly list Bagbin as the NDC candidate for **Nadowli West**, with 11,296 votes (50.5% of valid votes) [SRC-012]. For 2008, Parliament's indexed result reports 12,997 votes (61.9%) [SRC-011], while a directly inspected Modern Ghana table reports 12,902 (61.93%) [SRC-061]. The candidate-level discrepancy is unresolved; the public timeline preserves both claims and their evidence grades. The current Parliament MP directory lists a different member for Nadowli/Kaleo and does not provide Bagbin’s retired-member profile [SRC-004]. The inspected sources do not provide a complete authoritative list of constituencies or election returns across his career.

### Parliamentary roles

* **Speaker:** Parliament identifies his tenure as starting 2021-01-07 and ongoing [SRC-002]. Parliamentary news confirms he presided over House business in the Ninth Parliament, including on 2026-09-29 [SRC-017].
* **Previous leadership:** On 2025-01-23 the incoming Majority Leader, speaking in the House, referred to Bagbin’s progression from Minority Leader to Majority Leader, service in the Executive, Deputy Speaker, and Speaker [SRC-025]. This is an attributed statement in Hansard, not a complete appointment record; exact appointment dates and the named executive portfolio are not given by that passage.
* **Committee service:** The downloaded 2013 Plant Breeder’s Bill report is signed by Bagbin as Chairperson of the Committee on Constitutional, Legal and Parliamentary Affairs [SRC-031]. The downloaded 2023 voter-registration report identifies him as Chairman of the Committee of the Whole [SRC-032], and the 23 June 2023 Standing Orders report identifies him as Speaker and Chairman of that Committee [SRC-057]. These documents establish those report-specific roles but not a complete committee-service history.
* **Election as Speaker:** The Office page establishes the start of his Speakership on 2021-01-07 [SRC-002]. The Speaker-election ballot, vote total, opponent, and any later election/re-election details were not independently verified from a directly inspected contemporaneous parliamentary record in this investigation.

### Gaps in the chronology

The period from first election through the election of Speaker is not fully reconstructed election-by-election. Parliament’s accessible history pages do not expose an old Bagbin member profile. A downloaded 2004 results PDF establishes one Nadowli West result, while 2008 figures conflict between an indexed Parliament result and a secondary table. The source set does not settle the exact constituency name sequence, results for all elections, dates for leadership/executive assignments, the duration of Deputy Speakership, or either Speaker-election ballot record. These are research gaps, not assertions that records do not exist elsewhere.

## D. Extracted parliamentary records and activities

### Speaker decisions, communications, and addresses

1. **Committee reports:** downloaded/processed reports directly identify Bagbin as chairperson of the Committee on Constitutional, Legal and Parliamentary Affairs (2013), Chairman of the Committee of the Whole (2023), and Speaker/Chairman of the Standing Orders Committee (2023) [SRC-031, SRC-032, SRC-057]. These specific signed reports do not establish a complete committee-service history or individual bill sponsorship.
2. **29 September 2026 inadmissibility ruling:** the Parliament report says the motion proposed an inquiry into recent narcotics seizures. Bagbin ruled it inadmissible under the listed Standing Orders, referring to ongoing investigations, pending judicial proceedings, the House’s oversight functions, and possible overlap with matters for a competent court [SRC-017].
3. **27 November 2025 Kpandai communication:** Parliament reports Bagbin’s view that an MP remained entitled to participate during a seven-day stay period after service of a High Court decision; the report reproduces legal rule language and his explanation [SRC-026].
4. **31 January 2025 Appointments Committee communication:** title and date are listed on the official communications page; full text was not available at the inspected link [SRC-007]. A separately archived 2021 communication is downloadable from the Parliamentary repository [SRC-015], but it is not the same document.
5. **2024 Standing Orders:** Bagbin’s opening remarks at an orientation discuss adaptation to updated House procedure. The revised Orders were stated to be effective 2 January 2024. This is an address about institutional rules, not a personal legislative sponsorship record [SRC-022].
6. **2025 Rabat intervention:** official Speaker speech listing identifies the meeting, date, and his CoSPAL role. The speech attachment/full text was not exposed in the page extraction [SRC-006].
7. **2026 decentralisation forum:** speech/report calls for inclusive decentralisation and names Parliament TV and committee-sitting broadcasts as transparency mechanisms [SRC-019].
8. **2026 Supreme Court anniversary lecture:** the downloaded full text was processed alongside Parliament’s news summary; it addresses constitutional democracy, rights, institutional relations, judicial accountability, and the Court’s future role [SRC-016, SRC-021].

### Legislative contributions, questions, motions, votes, and committees

No complete speaker-specific ledger of bills, motions sponsored, questions, individual votes, committee memberships, reports, or Hansard interventions was extracted. The official reports archive is paginated (the initial page showed pages 1–42) and contains daily Hansards. The Order Paper archive is paginated (initial listing showed pages 1–27); Votes & Proceedings results surfaced pages 28 and 50 in search results. These archives contain broad House business, but the lists alone do not document Bagbin’s participation in each listed sitting, and I did not download and search all underlying Hansards/orders/proceedings. Including every item in those archives as a Bagbin record would be misleading.

Evidence labels:

* **Directly inspected:** page text, archive listing, or repository metadata was fetched and reviewed.
* **Search-index result:** Parliament’s or the public search provider’s indexed excerpt surfaced the page; its entire page/document was not independently inspected. These entries are leads, not complete verification.
* **Route unavailable:** a link was indexed but direct fetch returned 404. It is listed to preserve the discovery failure.
* **Summary/attribution:** unless quotation marks are used, statements are concise summaries attributed to the Parliament page/document. The report quotes only short wording actually visible in the source extraction.

## F. Research coverage, methods, and gaps

### Search route and sections examined

1. Began at the requested order-paper search URL: `https://www.parliament.gh/search?for=order%20papers`. Its returned page showed selected 2016 and 2019 order papers and Office of the Speaker/history links. It did not constitute a comprehensive query of all records.
2. Followed Parliament navigation to Office of the Speaker, Leadership of Parliament, MP directory, Speaker communications, Speaker speeches, Official Reports/Hansard, Order Papers, and Votes & Proceedings.
3. Inspected the first current-member directory listing and its pagination structure. Bagbin is not in that current MP roster, which lists current Ninth Parliament members. Direct Parliament site searches for “Alban Bagbin” and “Bagbin” returned only the Office of the Speaker link; a search-indexed legacy leadership route (`/mps?leadership`) returned 404 on direct request. These are recorded as search limitations, not evidence that no further records exist.
4. Searched official-site indexed material with name variants: “Alban Sumana Kingsford Bagbin,” “Alban Kingsford Sumana Bagbin,” “Alban S.K. Bagbin,” “Bagbin,” “Speaker Bagbin,” “Nadowli West,” and role terms such as Speaker, Deputy Speaker, Majority Leader, Minority Leader, plus years and terms.
5. Followed Parliamentary repository results for election records, 2007 remarks, Speaker communications, and the 2026 Supreme Court lecture. Parliament’s repository and older IR host are treated as Parliament-hosted sources, distinct from the main website.
6. Reviewed selected 2023–2026 Speaker news items and procedural actions surfaced by search. The current source inventory includes duplicate reporting of the 2026 decentralisation forum and Supreme Court lecture as independent source rows.

### Access and processing counts

* **61 unique source URLs** are catalogued across the initial dataset and enrichment delta. **42 pages/items** were directly fetched and inspected; **18** remain indexed/search-only; one indexed legacy route returned 404.
* **Eleven downloadable documents/files** were identified. Nine were downloaded and text-extracted or visually reviewed: 2004 election results, a 2000 presidential-results page, 2007 closing remarks, 2021 formal communication, 2026 Supreme Court lecture, 2013 Plant Breeder’s Bill report, 2023 voter-registration report, 2023 Standing Orders report, and the 2025 Constitution Amendment Bill. The 2008 Parliament election-result route returned HTML rather than a PDF, and IPU proceedings returned HTTP 403; those two remain unprocessed.
* The official reports listing initially exposed pages 1–42; the order-paper listing exposed pages 1–27. A Votes & Proceedings archive page reference appeared at page 28 and a search result at page 50. This demonstrates that records extend beyond the pages inspected.
* The site produced occasional route errors and search requests were rate-limited during later searches. No authentication or access controls were bypassed.

### Unresolved issues

* No direct contemporary official vote record of Bagbin’s 2021 election as Speaker, or a direct 2025 Speaker-election tally, was retrieved.
* No complete primary-source list of his seven pre-Speaker terms, historical constituencies, election results, committee memberships, or leadership/executive appointment dates was found. The 2008 Nadowli West vote totals remain conflicting.
* Hansards and proceedings were not comprehensively downloaded, OCR-searched, or coded; exhaustive coverage of interventions, questions, votes, and legislative items remains outstanding.
* The 2025 communications and speeches index pages show titles but do not expose full text in the fetched representation.
* The 2021 repository record’s issue date is 2021-07-21 while repository access/ingest metadata in its full record is dated 2026-07-07; those are distinct dates, not a contradiction in publication date.
* News item CO=284 and CO=286 are separate URLs carrying substantially repeated reporting of the 7 March 2026 forum; both are preserved in the inventory.
* Search-index publication timestamps occasionally differ from page-stated dates or appear to reflect indexing/update times. The page-stated event/publication date is preferred when visible; discrepancies are noted in the dataset.
